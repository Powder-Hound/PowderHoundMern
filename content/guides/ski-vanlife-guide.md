# The PowAlert Ski Vanlife & Camping Guide 2026/27

A practical reference for skiing from a van, truck camper, or RV in North America this season: where you can legally sleep at the mountain, what it costs, where to go when the lot says no, and how to stay warm, powered, and safe.

**Policies checked: September 27, 2026.** Most resorts had not yet posted 2026/27 winter overnight rates on that date. Where a page still showed summer 2026 or 2025/26 details, we say so. Confirm with the resort before you drive.

How to read this guide:

- **Fact** statements carry an inline link to the page we read.
- Where we could not confirm a rule or price on a primary source, we cut it.
- **Inference** is labeled as such. It is our reading of the facts, not a policy.

---

## 1. A short history, and why the rules look the way they do

Sleeping at the ski area is older than the word "vanlife." Warren Miller and Ward Baker spent the late 1940s living in a small unheated teardrop trailer in the Sun Valley parking lot, skiing every day and cooking outside. Miller wrote that the resort's manager let them stay as "local color" ([Flathead Beacon, "Winter of '47"](https://flatheadbeacon.com/2014/11/06/winter-47/); [Idaho Mountain Express, "My Travels with Ward Baker"](https://www.mtexpress.com/area_history/warren-miller-column---my-travels-with-ward-baker/article_6e1642bc-01fa-11e8-b1d1-279f4e10534f.html)). Two trailers in a lot count as color. Two hundred need a snow-removal plan.

That is roughly what happened. As overnight use grew, many destination resorts moved from tolerating it to banning it or allowing it only in designated spots. Pitkin County (Aspen) voted in January 2026 to ban camping in vehicles on unincorporated roads and rights-of-way ([Aspen Times](https://www.aspentimes.com/news/pitkin-county-commissioners-approve-ban-on-roadside-camping/)). The recurring reasons on resort pages are snow removal, emergency access, and the lack of sanitation.

The picture is not all bans. Many mid-size and independent areas now sell capped, reservable, self-contained overnight sites with quiet hours and stay limits. Examples are White Pass, Mission Ridge, Grand Targhee, Taos, Mt. Bachelor, and Big White (see the table below).

**Inference:** The pattern in our table is consistent. Resorts with large lodging operations tend to prohibit overnight stays. Independents and mid-size areas are more likely to sell them.

## 2. Resort-by-resort overnight policy (North America)

**Key to the Status column**

- **Allowed:** overnight stays in a vehicle are permitted in a designated area, usually self-contained only.
- **Restricted:** overnight parking is possible, but sleeping in the vehicle is prohibited or unclear, or you need prior authorization.
- **Banned:** no overnight parking or camping in resort lots.
- **Not posted:** no overnight policy found on the resort's own site on the check date.

"Self-contained" means onboard toilet and water, with all waste packed out. Unless the Cost column says otherwise, assume no hookups, no water, and no dump station. Prices are in US dollars for US resorts. Canadian resort prices are shown as C$, on the assumption that Canadian resort sites list Canadian dollars (inference). All pages were checked September 27, 2026.

## 3. Legal alternatives near the mountain

When the lot says no, these are the options. All of them have rules, and many close or thin out in winter.

### National forest and BLM land (general rules)

- **Forest Service stay limits are set forest by forest, not nationally.** Bridger-Teton (Jackson area) allows 14 days in any 30-day period. After that you must move at least 5 air miles and stay away from the site for 30 days. RVs there must be self-contained, and graywater goes to designated dump stations ([Bridger-Teton NF](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r04/bridger-teton/recreation/camping-cabins)). Monarch's parking page cites a 14-days-in-30 federal limit (36 CFR 261.58(a)) for the adjacent forest ([skimonarch.com](https://skimonarch.com/parking/)).
- **BLM dispersed camping is generally limited to 14 days in any 28-day period.** After that you must move, often at least 25 to 30 miles. Limits vary by field office ([BLM](https://www.blm.gov/programs/recreation/camping)).
- **Inference:** In winter, many forest roads are unplowed or closed. A spot that works in September may be unreachable in January. Check the local ranger district and its motor vehicle use map before relying on dispersed sites.

### By cluster

**Wasatch (Cottonwood Canyons).** Sleeping in a parked vehicle is prohibited within 1/2 mile of any road in the Cottonwood Canyons, except in designated campgrounds. The reason is Salt Lake City watershed rules, and UDOT says the order is "effective 100% of the time." Canyon campgrounds (Spruces, Jordan Pines, Redman, Tanners Flat, Albion Basin) open and close by season. The Town of Alta has three designated overnight parking areas, and overnight parking elsewhere in town is prohibited ([UDOT Cottonwood Canyons](https://cottonwoodcanyons.udot.utah.gov/travel-resources/)). Plan to sleep in the valley.

**I-70 / Summit County.** Copper says camping is not permitted on the resort. It names Tiger Run Resort for RV sites year-round and says Frisco and Dillon campgrounds are summer-only ([coppercolorado.com](https://www.coppercolorado.com/plan-your-trip/getting-here/parking)). Tiger Run lists 371 full-hookup sites, open year-round, five miles from Breckenridge ([tigerrunresort.com](https://www.tigerrunresort.com/rv-sites)). Breckenridge prohibits sleeping in vehicles in any town lot or street ([Breck Park](https://www.breckpark.com/free-parking)). A January 2026 report says overnight roadside parking along Loveland Pass generally is not allowed ([Unofficial Networks, secondary](https://unofficialnetworks.com/2026/01/09/car-camp-loveland-pass-colorado/)).

**Aspen / Roaring Fork.** Pitkin County banned camping and overnight occupancy in vehicles on unincorporated public roads and rights-of-way in January 2026 ([Aspen Times](https://www.aspentimes.com/news/pitkin-county-commissioners-approve-ban-on-roadside-camping/)).

**Jackson Hole.** The resort points campers to Grand Teton National Park campgrounds, Bridger-Teton dispersed camping, and private RV parks ([jacksonhole.com](https://www.jacksonhole.com/camping)). In winter, Jackson Hole Campground (Fireside Resort) offers electric-only sites without water or sewer hookups ([jacksonholecampground.com](http://jacksonholecampground.com/); [Fireside Resort](https://www.firesidejacksonhole.com/jackson-hole-rv-camping.html)).

**Mammoth.** Mammoth Mountain RV Park says it is open year-round and lists winter RV sites, a dump station, and propane fill ([mammothrv.com](https://mammothrv.com/amenities.htm); [winter page](https://mammothrv.com/winter-rv-camping-mammoth.htm)).

**Oregon Cascades.** Oregon requires a Sno-Park permit in designated winter recreation areas from November 1 to April 30: $4 daily, $9 for three days, $25 annual ([ODOT](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/vehicle/sno_park_permits.aspx)). Mt. Hood Meadows requires one in all its lots during that window ([skihood.com](https://www.skihood.com/explore/RV-Parking)).

**Washington Cascades.** When the White Pass lot is full, the resort suggests Cascade Peaks (27 miles west) or Silver Beach (9 miles east) ([skiwhitepass.com](https://skiwhitepass.com/lodging-and-dining/rv-parking)). Mission Ridge suggests Confluence State Park and Blu-Shastin RV Park ([missionridge.com](https://www.missionridge.com/rv-overnight-parking/)).

**Revelstoke.** The resort lists Boulder Mountain Resort, The Reverie, and Smokey Bear for winter camping ([revelstokemountainresort.com](https://www.revelstokemountainresort.com/discover/lodging/rv-parks-camping/)).

**Banff and Lake Louise.** In Banff National Park, camping is allowed only in designated campgrounds, with a camping permit and a park pass. Maximum stay is 14 nights. Two campgrounds are open year-round: Tunnel Mountain Village II (electrical sites, 15/30/50A) and Lake Louise Hard-sided (15/30/50A, hard-sided units only). Both list a dumping station ([Parks Canada](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/ab/banff/activ/camping)).

**Vermont and New Hampshire.** Sunday River points to Stony Brook Campground, about five miles away ([sundayriver.com](https://www.sundayriver.com/policies-and-safety/resort-policies)). In Franconia Notch, the NH State Parks-run Cannon Mountain RV Park takes reservations year-round ([cannonmt.com](https://www.cannonmt.com/winter-camping-rv-park)). Gifford Woods State Park near Killington lists 22 tent/RV sites and a trailer sanitary station, but its posted season runs from the Friday of Memorial Day weekend through the third weekend in October, so it is not a ski-season option ([vtstateparks.com](https://www.vtstateparks.com/parks/gifford-woods)).

### Retail lots and membership networks

- **Walmart.** Walmart's corporate FAQ says it permits RV parking "as we are able." Permission comes from individual store managers based on space and local laws, and Walmart asks you to contact store management first ([Ask Walmart](https://corporate.walmart.com/askwalmart)). **Inference:** Many mountain towns restrict overnight parking by ordinance, so expect store-level refusals near resorts.
- **Cabela's / Bass Pro Shops.** We found no published corporate overnight policy. Confirm overnight parking with the specific store before you go.
- **Harvest Hosts.** An annual membership for self-contained RVs to stay overnight at farms, wineries, breweries, and similar hosts. Plans are $99/year (Classic), $169 (with Boondockers Welcome), or $179 (All Access). There is no camping fee. Harvest Hosts suggests supporting the host with a purchase of about $30 per one-night stay ([Harvest Hosts guide](https://www.harvesthosts.com/blog/what-is-harvest-hosts-a-complete-guide-to-how-it-works); [how it works](https://www.harvesthosts.com/how-it-works)). **Inference:** Winter availability near ski towns is thinner than in summer.
- **Hipcamp.** A booking marketplace for campsites on private land ([hipcamp.com](https://www.hipcamp.com/)). Confirm winter access and plowing with the host before you go.

## 4. Winter van and camping gear checklist

This list is guidance, not a set of facts. Items tied to a specific rule or spec carry a link.

**Traction and recovery**

- 3PMSF-rated winter tires. Utah's Cottonwood Canyons rule requires 3PMSF tires on 2WD vehicles when the traction law is active, and at least 5/32 in tread for all vehicles ([UDOT](https://cottonwoodcanyons.udot.utah.gov/traction-law/))
- Chains sized for your tires, plus repair links and a headlamp ([Caltrans](https://dot.ca.gov/travel/winter-driving-tips)). Grand Targhee requires campers to bring their own chains ([grandtarghee.com](https://www.grandtarghee.com/plan-your-trip/lodging-options/winter-camping))
- Full-size snow shovel, snow brush, and roof rake
- Traction boards, tow strap, and gloves
- Spare key on your person ([Caltrans](https://dot.ca.gov/travel/winter-driving-tips))

**Safety**

- A battery-powered CO alarm near where you sleep ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/about/index.html))
- A propane detector if you carry propane, mounted per the maker's instructions ([PERC](https://cloudinary.propane.com/images/v1756137316/website-media/PRC-101063D-Propane-Safety-Booklet_digital-1_144644b80f7/PRC-101063D-Propane-Safety-Booklet_digital-1_144644b80f7.pdf))
- ABC fire extinguisher and first-aid kit
- Avalanche transceiver, probe, and shovel if you tour
- Paper map, and offline maps on your phone

**Warmth and sleep**

- Sleeping bag rated below the expected overnight low
- Closed-cell pad under an insulated mattress
- Window insulation panels (these also reduce condensation)
- Dry base layers stored separately from wet ski gear

**Water and waste**

- Insulated or heated freshwater storage, or containers you can bring inside
- A cassette or composting toilet. Most allowed lots require self-contained vehicles, and Killington requires your own portable toilet or approved system ([killington.com](https://www.killington.com/camping))
- Trash bags. You pack everything out at Mt. Baker, Grand Targhee, and Summit ([mtbaker.us](https://www.mtbaker.us/getting-here/overnight-parking/op-occupied-vehicles/); [grandtarghee.com](https://www.grandtarghee.com/plan-your-trip/lodging-options/winter-camping); [summitatsnoqualmie.com](https://www.summitatsnoqualmie.com/overnight-parking))

**Paperwork**

- Printed reservation or permit. Mt. Bachelor asks for your confirmation in the windshield ([mtbachelor.com](https://www.mtbachelor.com/amenities-groups/overnight-rv-camping/)). Targhee requires the permit to be visible at all times ([grandtarghee.com](https://www.grandtarghee.com/plan-your-trip/lodging-options/winter-camping)). Sugarloaf requires your name and phone number in the window ([sugarloaf.com](https://www.sugarloaf.com/parking-and-shuttles))
- Oregon Sno-Park permit where required ([ODOT](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/vehicle/sno_park_permits.aspx))

## 5. Power and heat

### Heat

- **Diesel and gasoline air heaters.** These are the common choice for vans because they burn fuel outside the living space and blow heated air inside. Webasto, for example, sells the Air Top 2000 STC as a 2 kW diesel air heater for RVs ([Webasto](https://www.webasto.com/en-int/heating/air-heater/air-top-2000-stc.html)). Current draw, fuel use, and altitude performance vary by model. Use your unit's manual rather than forum figures (we did not verify model-specific numbers for this guide).
- **Exhaust clearance.** Snow can bury exhaust outlets overnight. NFPA warns to make sure a running vehicle's exhaust pipe is not covered with snow ([NFPA](https://www.nfpa.org/education-and-research/home-fire-safety/carbon-monoxide)). **Inference:** The same risk applies to heater exhausts at snow level. Clear them before sleeping and after heavy snowfall.
- **Propane.** Propane is heavier than air and can settle near the floor. Propane detectors are separate devices from CO detectors, though combination units exist ([PERC Propane Safety Guide](https://cloudinary.propane.com/images/v1756137316/website-media/PRC-101063D-Propane-Safety-Booklet_digital-1_144644b80f7/PRC-101063D-Propane-Safety-Booklet_digital-1_144644b80f7.pdf)). **Inference:** In a van, that makes floor-level leaks the main worry. Never use unvented indoor propane heaters while you sleep.
- **CO alarms.** Install battery-operated or battery-backup CO detectors near every sleeping area. People who are asleep can die of CO poisoning before they notice symptoms ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/about/index.html)).

### Batteries in the cold

- **Lithium iron phosphate (LiFePO4) must not be charged below freezing without protection.** Victron's Lithium Battery Smart line specifies charging from +5°C to +50°C (41°F to 122°F) and discharging from -20°C to +50°C. For the 50 Ah model, Victron lists usable capacity of 50 Ah at 25°C, 40 Ah at 0°C, and 25 Ah at -20°C ([Victron technical data](https://www.victronenergy.com/media/pg/Lithium_Battery_Smart/en/technical-data.html)). Other brands differ. Check your battery's datasheet, or use self-heating batteries or an insulated, heated battery location.
- **Sizing method (worked example, not a spec).** Add up daily watt-hours as watts times hours for each load. Then add margin for cold-weather capacity loss and short winter charging windows. Say you assume a 30 W heater fan-and-glow average for 10 hours (300 Wh), a 40 W fridge average over 24 hours (960 Wh), and 100 Wh of lights and phones. That is about 1,360 Wh per day. At the Victron 0°C figure (80% of rated capacity), a 12.8 V bank would need about 133 Ah of usable capacity for one day with no charging. All load figures here are illustrative assumptions. Measure your own with a battery monitor.
- **Solar in winter.** **Inference:** Short days, low sun angle, and snow-covered panels cut winter solar output sharply. Treat solar as a supplement, and plan on alternator charging (DC-DC charger) or shore power for multi-day stays. Mt. Bachelor (50/30/20A), Crystal (30/50A), Willamette Pass, Whitewater (30A at six sites), and Jackson Hole Campground (Fireside) winter sites are among the places with electric hookups ([mtbachelor.com](https://www.mtbachelor.com/amenities-groups/overnight-rv-camping/); [crystalmountainresort.com](https://www.crystalmountainresort.com/things-to-do/overnight-and-rv-parking); [willamettepass.ski](https://www.willamettepass.ski/overnight-parking/); [whitewatermountainresort.com](https://whitewatermountainresort.com/accommodation/camping-at-whitewater/); [jacksonholecampground.com](http://jacksonholecampground.com/)).

### Generators

Generator rules are site-specific:

- **Mt. Bachelor:** 7am-10pm, under 60 decibels ([mtbachelor.com](https://www.mtbachelor.com/amenities-groups/overnight-rv-camping/))
- **Killington:** until 10pm ([killington.com](https://www.killington.com/camping))
- **Brundage:** none 10pm-8am ([brundage.com](https://brundage.com/winter-camping/))
- **Mission Ridge:** only quiet generators during 11pm-8am quiet hours ([missionridge.com](https://www.missionridge.com/rv-overnight-parking/))
- **Banff National Park campgrounds:** 9am-10:30am and 5pm-7pm only ([Parks Canada](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/ab/banff/activ/camping))

## 7. Safety and cold

### Carbon monoxide

CO is odorless and colorless. Each year, more than 400 Americans die from unintentional CO poisoning not linked to fires ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/about/index.html)). Symptoms include headache, dizziness, weakness, upset stomach, vomiting, chest pain, and confusion ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/about/index.html)). Keep a working alarm near your bed. Clear snow from engine and heater exhausts. Do not idle the engine for heat with the tailpipe buried ([NFPA](https://www.nfpa.org/education-and-research/home-fire-safety/carbon-monoxide)).

### Chain and traction laws

- **Colorado Passenger Vehicle Traction Law.** CDOT can put it in effect on any state highway. When it's active, you need one of the following ([CDOT](https://www.codot.gov/travel/winter-driving/tractionlaw)):
  - AWD/4WD with M+S, winter, or manufacturer all-weather tires and at least 3/16 in tread, or
  - chains or an approved alternative traction device.
  - Under the Chain Law, every vehicle needs chains or an approved alternative.
  - Fines are $100 plus a $33 surcharge, or $500 plus $157 if noncompliance closes a lane.
- **Utah SR-210 and SR-190 (Little and Big Cottonwood).** Utah rule R920-6 sets a stricter "Class 3" standard for these canyons ([UDOT](https://cottonwoodcanyons.udot.utah.gov/traction-law/)):
  - At least 5/32 in tread when the canyon beacons flash.
  - AWD/4WD: M+S tires on all wheels at minimum.
  - 2WD: 3PMSF tires on all wheels, or traction devices on the drive wheels.
  - UDOT can impose requirements up to 24 hours before a storm.
  - There are no gas stations in either canyon.
- **California.** When chain controls are posted you must install chains or be cited. The speed limit under chain control is 25 or 30 mph ([Caltrans](https://dot.ca.gov/travel/winter-driving-tips)).
- **Washington.** From November 1 to April 1, vehicles over 10,000 lb GVW must carry chains at all times on these routes, among others ([WSDOT](https://wsdot.wa.gov/travel/commercial-vehicles/route-commercial-vehicle-restrictions/chain-requirements-vehicles-over-10000-pounds)):
  - I-90 over Snoqualmie
  - SR-2 over Stevens
  - SR-12 over White Pass
  - SR-410 (Crystal access)
  - SR-542 (Mt. Baker Highway)
  - **Inference:** Many larger RVs and heavy truck campers exceed 10,000 lb. Check your GVWR.
- **Mt. Baker access.** WSDOT does not plow the highway after 3:30pm, and Mt. Baker suggests arriving before 7pm ([mtbaker.us](https://www.mtbaker.us/getting-here/overnight-parking/op-occupied-vehicles/)).

### Avalanche closures and interlodge

Much of SR-210 and central Alta is exposed to avalanches. During periods of high hazard, the Alta Town Marshal may prohibit outdoor travel ("interlodge") under Town of Alta Ordinance 5-4-1 ([Town of Alta FAQ](https://www.townofalta.utah.gov/faq/); [alta.com](https://www.alta.com/stories/interlodge)). **Inference:** You are not allowed to sleep in a vehicle in Little Cottonwood anyway, but a canyon closure can still strand a van at the bottom or the top for hours. Carry food, water, fuel, and a phone charger, as UDOT advises ([UDOT](https://cottonwoodcanyons.udot.utah.gov/traction-law/)).

### Plowing and being asked to move

Many allowed lots require you to move for snow removal:

- **Mt. Baker** will contact you between 3pm and 5pm if you need to move ([mtbaker.us](https://www.mtbaker.us/getting-here/overnight-parking/op-occupied-vehicles/))
- **Grand Targhee** may plow overnight or early morning ([grandtarghee.com](https://www.grandtarghee.com/plan-your-trip/lodging-options/winter-camping))
- **Sugarloaf** requires you to move daily, preferably before 7:30am ([sugarloaf.com](https://www.sugarloaf.com/parking-and-shuttles))
- **Mt. Hood Meadows** closes RV Row on Tuesday nights for snow removal ([skihood.com](https://www.skihood.com/explore/RV-Parking))

This is why several resorts require the vehicle to be occupied overnight: Mt. Bachelor, Mission Ridge, Willamette Pass, and Schweitzer ([mtbachelor.com](https://www.mtbachelor.com/amenities-groups/overnight-rv-camping/); [missionridge.com](https://www.missionridge.com/rv-overnight-parking/); [willamettepass.ski](https://www.willamettepass.ski/overnight-parking/); [schweitzer.com](https://www.schweitzer.com/mountain-info/shuttles-parking)). Grand Targhee makes you responsible for your own tow if you get stuck ([grandtarghee.com](https://www.grandtarghee.com/plan-your-trip/lodging-options/winter-camping)).

### Frozen tanks and lines

**Inference, general practice:**

- Keep freshwater inside the heated envelope.
- Keep gray tanks near empty in hard cold, and use RV antifreeze in P-traps if a tank or drain is outside.
- Park so a slide-out or door does not face the prevailing snowdrift.
- Many hookups and fill points shut off water in freezing weather. Jackson Hole Campground's winter sites have no water or sewer ([jacksonholecampground.com](http://jacksonholecampground.com/)), and Mt. Baker has no potable water after hours ([mtbaker.us](https://www.mtbaker.us/getting-here/overnight-parking/op-occupied-vehicles/)).

## 8. Etiquette

These norms come from the rules resorts publish. They are also the reasons overnight programs survive or get cancelled.

- **Reserve, then show up or cancel.** Mt. Bachelor gives no refunds and penalizes no-shows. Crystal flags and cancels resold reservations ([mtbachelor.com](https://www.mtbachelor.com/amenities-groups/overnight-rv-camping/); [crystalmountainresort.com](https://www.crystalmountainresort.com/things-to-do/overnight-and-rv-parking)).
- **Stay inside your space.** Mission Ridge asks for a 30-degree back-in angle and no more than 5 ft on either side. Summit asks for back-in around the lot perimeter. Hood Meadows allows one extra vehicle in front of the RV within 40 ft ([missionridge.com](https://www.missionridge.com/rv-overnight-parking/); [summitatsnoqualmie.com](https://www.summitatsnoqualmie.com/overnight-parking); [skihood.com](https://www.skihood.com/explore/RV-Parking)).
- **No saving spots.** Mission Ridge prohibits it ([missionridge.com](https://www.missionridge.com/rv-overnight-parking/)).
- **Respect quiet hours.** These are commonly 10pm or 11pm to 7am or 8am. Examples: Crystal 10pm-8am, Summit 11pm-8am, Killington 11pm-7am, Brundage 10pm-8am ([crystalmountainresort.com](https://www.crystalmountainresort.com/things-to-do/overnight-and-rv-parking); [summitatsnoqualmie.com](https://www.summitatsnoqualmie.com/overnight-parking); [killington.com](https://www.killington.com/camping); [brundage.com](https://brundage.com/winter-camping/)).
- **Fires.** Wood fires are banned at Mt. Bachelor, Crystal, Hoodoo, and Summit, and propane fire pits are generally the only option ([mtbachelor.com](https://www.mtbachelor.com/amenities-groups/overnight-rv-camping/); [crystalmountainresort.com](https://www.crystalmountainresort.com/things-to-do/overnight-and-rv-parking); [hoodoo.com](https://hoodoo.com/rates/rv-rates/); [summitatsnoqualmie.com](https://www.summitatsnoqualmie.com/overnight-parking)). Sugarloaf bans even contained devices like Solo Stoves ([sugarloaf.com](https://www.sugarloaf.com/parking-and-shuttles)).
- **Pack out everything, and never discharge tanks on site** ([summitatsnoqualmie.com](https://www.summitatsnoqualmie.com/overnight-parking); [schweitzer.com](https://www.schweitzer.com/mountain-info/shuttles-parking)).
- **Keep alcohol inside the vehicle in Washington lots.** Summit and Mission Ridge cite state law ([summitatsnoqualmie.com](https://www.summitatsnoqualmie.com/overnight-parking); [missionridge.com](https://www.missionridge.com/rv-overnight-parking/)).
- **Food storage.** In bear country, store food in a closed vehicle ([grandtarghee.com](https://www.grandtarghee.com/plan-your-trip/lodging-options/winter-camping)).
- **No skiing outside operating hours or during avalanche control.** Mission Ridge prohibits it ([missionridge.com](https://www.missionridge.com/rv-overnight-parking/)).
- **Buy the product.** Killington's lot is only for guests with a valid mountain product ([killington.com](https://www.killington.com/camping)). **Inference:** Spending at the resort you sleep at is the strongest argument for keeping these programs open.
